Confirmation Bias

Confirmation Bias means paying more attention to information that supports what you already believe while ignoring information that disagrees with it.

An example of how this may show up in my life is wanting to buy a certain brand and only looking at positive reviews instead of checking the negative reviews too.

This bias can lead to a poor financial decision by causing me to overlook problems with a product and spend money on something that is not worth the price.

Endowment Effect

Endowment Effect means valuing something more just because you own it.

An example of how this may show up in my life is keeping an old game or device and thinking it is worth more than someone would actually pay for it.

This bias can lead to a poor financial decision by making me hold on to something I do not use instead of selling it for a reasonable price.

Loss Aversion

Loss Aversion means feeling the pain of losing money more strongly than the happiness of gaining the same amount.

An example of how this may show up in my life is refusing to sell something I bought, even when I no longer need it, because I do not want to feel like I lost money.

This bias can lead to a poor financial decision by making me keep spending time or money on something that is no longer a good choice, just to avoid admitting a loss.

My Money Motto

“I will save as much as I can, while spending what I need to.”
